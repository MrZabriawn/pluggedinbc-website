Government Contracting

One of the Biggest Opportunities You Might Be Missing

Federal, state, and local governments spend billions annually on goods and services. Small, historically disadvantaged businesses have real advantages in this market — if they know how to compete.

What Is Government Contracting?

Government contracting means selling your products or services to federal agencies (like the Department of Defense, VA, or SBA), state agencies (like PENNDOT or PA Department of General Services), or local governments (like Beaver County or municipal school districts).

Governments are legally required to spend a percentage of their contracts with small and disadvantaged businesses — which means they're actively looking for businesses like yours.

📋 Steps to Government Contracting

1. Get registered in SAM.gov (federal) or PA Supplier Portal (state)
2. Obtain your NAICS codes (industry classification codes)
3. Pursue relevant certifications (MBE, WBE, DBE, SDB, SDVOSB)
4. Identify bid opportunities on USASpending.gov, PA eBid, or local portals
5. Submit competitive proposals — PluggedInBC helps you every step of the way

Supplier Diversity

Certifications That Open Doors

Supplier diversity certifications signal to government agencies and corporations that your business meets specific ownership and control criteria — unlocking set-aside contracts and preferred supplier opportunities.

MBE
Minority Business Enterprise

For businesses at least 51% owned and controlled by racial or ethnic minorities. Opens set-aside contracting opportunities with government and corporate diversity programs.

WBE
Women Business Enterprise

For businesses at least 51% owned and controlled by women. Recognized by government agencies and private corporations with supplier diversity goals.

DBE
Disadvantaged Business Enterprise

Federal certification for small businesses owned by socially and economically disadvantaged individuals. Required for certain federally-funded transportation and infrastructure contracts.

SDB
Small Disadvantaged Business

SBA designation for small businesses owned by socially and economically disadvantaged individuals. Provides bidding advantages on federal contracts.

Additional Certifications

We also help businesses explore SDVOSB (Service-Disabled Veteran-Owned Small Business), VOSB (Veteran-Owned Small Business), HUBZone certification, and Pennsylvania-specific certifications. Each comes with different eligibility requirements and contracting advantages — we help you identify which ones make sense for your business.

Capital Access

Funding Options for Growing Businesses

Capital is a growth accelerant — when you have the right amount at the right time from the right source. We help you navigate the full landscape of funding options available to Beaver County businesses.

🏦

SBA Loans

The Small Business Administration guarantees loans through approved lenders, making it easier for small businesses to access capital with favorable terms. SBA 7(a) loans, SBA 504 loans, and SBA microloans each serve different needs — from working capital to equipment to real estate. PluggedInBC helps you understand your options and prepare a strong application package.

🌱

CDFI Microloans & Loans

Community Development Financial Institutions (CDFIs) are mission-driven lenders specifically designed to serve underserved entrepreneurs. They offer more flexible credit requirements than traditional banks and often provide technical assistance alongside capital. We have established relationships with CDFI partners serving the Beaver County region.

🏛️

PA DCED Grants & Programs

The Pennsylvania Department of Community and Economic Development administers multiple grant, loan, and incentive programs for small businesses. Eligibility varies by program and business type. Our advisors stay current on available DCED programs and help eligible businesses prepare competitive applications.

💸

Microloans

For businesses needing smaller amounts of capital — typically $5,000 to $50,000 — microloans from community lenders and CDFIs can provide working capital, equipment financing, or inventory funding with more accessible qualification requirements than traditional bank loans. Ideal for startups and early-stage businesses building credit history.

👥

Community Banks & Credit Unions

Local financial institutions often have more flexibility and community investment motivation than large national banks. We maintain relationships with community banks and credit unions in the Beaver County area and can make warm referrals based on your specific financing needs and credit profile.

🔗

Other Capital Sources

The capital ecosystem includes angel investors, business development organizations, foundation grants, crowdfunding, revenue-based financing, and more. Every business has a different capital profile — and we help you find the sources that fit your stage, industry, and risk profile without taking on more than you can manage.
